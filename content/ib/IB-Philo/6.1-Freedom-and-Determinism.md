## Main questions:

>Are we actually *choosing* anything, or is everything already *determined* by someone or something else?

>Can it be that both are true at the same time?

>The document tries to explore different philosophers' approaches

> [!tip] Quick summary
> **- Spinoza:** No free will. Everything happens because of something else (domino).\
> **- Kant:** You’re free if you act morally and rationally.\
> **- Sartre:** You’re completely free, that’s why life is scary.\
> **- Dennett:** Determinism is real, but usable freedom still exists.
## Philosophers

### 1. **Baruch Spinoza**
==Hard Determinism==

Spinoza said that everything is already determined by nature, free will is an illusion.\
Every event has a cause - like a huge domino line\
	--> your decisions are reactions of your brain to earlier things that happened

**Main counter-argument:**\
If everything is predetermined, how can we blame people for doing bad things?\
	--> Spinoza's view undermines moral responsibility

### 2. **Immanuel Kant**
==Compatibilism==

Proposed that freedom and determinism can coexits.\
*'You're free as long as you act logically and morally'*\
Believes in natural laws, but! - humans are special because we have reason\
Basically:\
**You’re free if you act according to your morals, not your impulses.**

**Main counter-argument:**\
If my values are shaped by my world and the past then did I really choose anything?


### 3. Jean-Paul Sartre
==Existentialism, Radical Freedom==

“existence precedes essence”\
“humans are condemned to be free”

he is the drama queen saying:\
- you’re completely free\
- only you decide your life\
- there’s no meaning –> you have to create it\
- this is scary but face it

**Main counter-argument:**\
If everyone invents their own morals, that’s pretty messy. Without an objective moral framework \there is also a huge burden on the individual leading to despair.

### 4. Daniel Dennett
==Compatibilism + a Naturalistic Approach==

The universe is determined already. You are shaped by causes.\
but!! since you can do what you want, think, and act based on reasons - that should be interpreted as freedom

**Main counter-argument**\
Still doesn’t consider the moral implications –> same problem as Spinoza & Kant
